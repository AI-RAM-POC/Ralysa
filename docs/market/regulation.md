# Market Analysis: Regulation, data residency and buying constraints (GCC + EU AI Act)

> Phase 1 · Owner: market-analyst · Last updated: 2026-09-24 · Stream: regulation · Insight block: MA-301 to MA-310

> **Disclaimer:** This is market analysis to inform product and go-to-market decisions. It is not legal advice. Confirm every point with qualified counsel in each jurisdiction before relying on it in a contract, a tender response or a regulator submission.

**How to read the source tags**
- Every source link carries its access date. Unless a different date is given, it is "(accessed 2026-09-24)".
- **[>6m]** means the source document or the version cited is more than 6 months old (published before 2026-03-24). The rule may still be in force, but re-check it before relying on it.
- **[draft/pending]** means the instrument is a draft, is waiting on implementing rules, or its status is unclear.
- **[secondary]** means a law firm, vendor or press summary. It is weaker than a regulator's own text, and we could not read the primary document directly.
- **Assumption** or **Estimate** means no source was found. It is our reasoning only.

---

## Decision questions

1. **Which deployment models must be available at launch to sell to Gulf banks, telcos and government?** Options: multi-tenant SaaS, in-country SaaS, customer-dedicated cloud, on-prem, air-gapped.
2. **What must the Model Gateway do at launch so that regulated data never reaches a model in a non-approved location?** This covers in-country or local model support, residency routing and PII masking.
3. **Which certifications and compliance artefacts are needed before the first regulated contract, and which can follow?** How do regulator approvals change the length of the sales cycle?

---

## Executive summary

- **Gulf bank regulators effectively require in-country processing and prior regulator approval.** Qatar's QCB says PII and financial information must be "processed within Qatar only" and that QCB approval is needed before any cloud arrangement. SAMA requires approval before any cloud use, plus explicit approval for cloud outside KSA. CBUAE requires the bank's master records to stay in the UAE and approval before confidential data leaves. Banks will therefore accept only customer-dedicated in-country cloud or on-prem. See **MA-301**.
- **The real bottleneck is where the LLM runs, not where Ralysa runs.** On Azure, the only regional (in-country) frontier OpenAI deployment we found in the Gulf is *Regional Provisioned* in UAE North. Qatar Central and KSA are not listed. Claude on Bedrock in the Middle East uses *global* cross-region inference, so prompts leave the country. Local and open-weight models are therefore a launch requirement for Tier 3 (T3) data, not a later feature. See **MA-302**.
- **Government buyers go further.** Qatar keeps top-secret (C4) data out of the cloud entirely. Abu Dhabi targets 100% sovereign cloud for government. KSA links hosting to NDMO data classification and to CST cloud provider registration classes. Government deals need on-prem or air-gapped deployment, or delivery through a certified sovereign-cloud partner. See **MA-303**.
- **General privacy laws are more permissive than sector rules.** Qatar PDPPL Art. 15 allows cross-border transfers in principle. KSA allows transfers with SDAIA standard contractual clauses (SCCs) and a risk assessment. The UAE PDPL implementing regulations are *still not issued*. For non-regulated private enterprises, in-region SaaS with a data processing agreement (DPA), SCCs and masking is saleable. See **MA-304**.
- **AI-specific rules converge on features Ralysa already plans.** These are an AI system register or inventory, human oversight, audit, vendor due diligence, transparency notices and, for high-risk use, prior approval or certification. Sources: QCB AI Guideline, CBUAE AI Guidance Note (Feb 2026), DIFC Regulation 10, SDAIA and NCSA AI guidelines. Packaging these as a regulator-ready "evidence pack" is a differentiator. See **MA-305**.
- **The certification baseline:** ISO 27001 plus SOC 2 Type II are table stakes. Each segment then needs national control mappings: Qatar NIA, NCA ECC/CCC in KSA, UAE IA Regulation, ADHICS for Abu Dhabi health. ISO 42001 is increasingly the reference for AI governance. See **MA-306**.
- **Expect long sales cycles.** Regulator non-objection steps (for example, SAMA's 15-business-day minimum for material outsourcing) and fluid rules add months. These include the KSA localisation move from NCA to NDMO, the pending UAE PDPL regulations, and the new UAE Federal AI and Data Authority (June 2026). Residency must be policy-configurable, not hard-coded. See **MA-307** and **MA-308**.

## Sales-cycle implications

- **Banks.** The buyer usually needs a regulator step after choosing Ralysa: QCB approval of the cloud arrangement, SAMA approval or no-objection (at least 15 business days, 30 for foreign banks), or CBUAE non-objection. Add a vendor-risk review against the artefacts above. **Estimate:** 9–15 months from first meeting to production for a Gulf bank, versus 3–6 months for a non-regulated enterprise. This is not sourced. Validate it against pilot pipeline data.
- **On-prem or private cloud shortens the regulator step.** For example, SAMA §3.4.3 does not apply to private cloud. But it lengthens deployment and security review. It also moves the approval question onto the **model provider**, unless a local model is used.
- **Government.** Procurement runs through sovereign-cloud partners or local integrators, with national classification (C0–C4, NDMO levels) deciding what may be touched. **Assumption:** 12–24 months, and a local partner is required.
- **Telcos.** Driven by CII controls and internal policy. Telco Edition T3 packs (Revenue Assurance, Fraud, NOC) need local models. T1/T2 packs can start in-region, which allows a land-and-expand motion.
- **Regulatory churn is a sales risk and an opportunity.** Pending UAE PDPL regulations, the new UAE Federal AI and Data Authority, KSA's shift of localisation to NDMO and the DIFC Reg 10 certification rollout mean buyers want vendors that can re-configure policy without re-deploying.

## Recommendations

- **Positioning:** "Governed AI workspace that runs in your tenancy, in your country, with your models." Lead with customer-operated deployment and residency-aware model routing, not with SaaS. Use the evidence pack (MA-305) as the proof point against Copilot Studio and ChatGPT Enterprise. See MA-301, MA-302, MA-305.
- **Deployment models at launch** (answers decision question 1):
  1. **Customer-dedicated in-country cloud**: Terraform and Helm for Azure Qatar Central / UAE North, Google Doha / Dammam, AWS me-central-1, plus one sovereign cloud partner. **Must-have.**
  2. **On-prem Kubernetes with a bundled local-model runtime**: **Must-have** for banks, government and telco T3.
  3. **Air-gapped variant**, meaning (2) with no outbound calls, offline licence and offline updates: **plan it at launch, ship by Y1** for government C4 / Top Secret.
  4. **In-region multi-tenant SaaS**: for non-regulated mid-market only. **Nice-to-have at launch.**
  5. **Foreign-hosted multi-tenant SaaS**: **do not offer to Gulf regulated segments.**
- **Model Gateway at launch** (answers decision question 2): routing by data tier (T1–T3) × customer classification (Qatar C0–C4, NDMO levels) × allowed region list. Block by default any non-local call for T3 data. PII masking (Arabic and English) before any non-local call. Log the inference region for every call in the audit trail. Support vLLM and Ollama plus at least one Arabic sovereign or open model.
- **Pack priority:** first pilot should be **Technology pack at a telco or bank on T1/T2 data**, with a clear local-model path to Revenue Assurance, Fraud and NOC (T3). Defer HR (EU AI Act Annex III, strictest masking) and government (partner-dependent). See MA-303, MA-309, MA-310.
- **Pricing stance:** price dedicated and on-prem as enterprise platform fees plus seats. Local-model GPU capacity is customer-borne or partner-supplied. Keep a cheaper in-region SaaS SKU for non-regulated buyers (MA-304). Detailed pricing belongs to the pricing stream.
- **Roadmap changes:**
  - Raise spec §14 "Regional model availability" from Medium to **High** (MA-302).
  - Add REQ candidates: AI system register export; regulator evidence pack; tenant-wide agent kill-switch; inference-region audit field; classification-label-aware routing; exit/export bundle.
  - Start ISO 27001 now. Put SOC 2 Type I at launch and Type II in Y1. Deliver the Qatar NIA mapping and a QCB outsourcing-annex template before the first Qatar bank pilot.
