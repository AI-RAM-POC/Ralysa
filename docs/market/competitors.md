# Market Analysis: Competitive landscape

> Phase 1 · Owner: market-analyst · Last updated: 2026-09-24
> Stream: competitors. Segments, market sizing and regulation are covered in the other `docs/market/` stream files. Insight IDs in this file use the MA-100 block.

**How to read the evidence.** Every row cites a source with the date it was fetched. **Stale** means the source is more than 6 months old (published before 2026-03-24). **(secondary)** means a third-party blog, press piece or pricing aggregator was used because the vendor page was unavailable or didn't say. Treat those as weaker evidence. "Not published" means we found no vendor list price. "Not documented" means we found no vendor statement either way.

## Decision questions

1. **Where is Ralysa differentiated enough to win Gulf regulated enterprises** (telecom, banking, government in Qatar, UAE and KSA) against the hyperscaler suites and the new sovereign platforms?
2. **Which competitor capabilities are table stakes, and which are real gaps Ralysa can own?** This covers the Phase 1 scope: in-country or on-prem deployment, BYO model, MCP, department packs, Arabic/RTL and SSO-driven governance.
3. **Should Ralysa sell against the sovereign/regional players or through them?** Examples: G42/Core42, Humain, e&/Open Innovation AI, stc, Ooredoo.

## Executive summary

- **Global suites have fixed data residency in the UAE but not in Qatar, and KSA is only just arriving.** Microsoft 365 Copilot in-country processing lists the UAE for 2026. Qatar and KSA are not on the list, and Azure's Saudi East region only opens in November 2026. OpenAI has UAE storage and, since August 2026, UAE inference residency. Gemini Enterprise and first-party Claude offer no Middle East residency. → **MA-101**
- **Air-gapped or on-prem agent workspaces from the global vendors are close to non-existent.** Microsoft's disconnected sovereign stack does not include Copilot. Glean "customer-hosted" means a GCP or AWS VPC, not on-prem. Only Google (GDC air-gapped, models only) and regional players (Open Innovation AI, Arabic.AI, Core42 private cloud) sell truly isolated deployments. → **MA-102**
- **MCP and multi-model support are now table stakes, not differentiators.** Copilot Studio (GA), ChatGPT Enterprise (custom MCP apps), Gemini Enterprise (custom MCP servers plus Agent Gateway), Glean, Dust and Writer (MCP Gateway) all support MCP. Most offer a choice of Claude, GPT and Gemini. Ralysa must lead on *governed* MCP (per-group allowlists, approvals, audit), not on MCP itself. → **MA-103**
- **Sovereign Gulf platforms are infrastructure- and model-first. They are weak on a department-ready, Claude-Code-grade agent workspace.** Core42 Compass, stc/SCCC, Ooredoo/Rafay and the e&/OI Sovereign AI Platform sell GPUs, models, an API or an "agent factory". Humain One is the only one pitching department agents (HR, finance, procurement). → **MA-104, MA-105**
- **Arabic support is uneven.** The models handle Arabic well (Claude Sonnet 4.5 scores 97.2% of its English performance, and M365 Copilot lists Arabic). But enterprise-search and workspace layers lag: Glean's Arabic support is "Early access", and Dust and Writer don't document it. Arabic-first document handling plus an RTL UI is a credible edge, but only against the global players. → **MA-106**
- **Pricing splits into a low seat fee plus metered usage.** Claude Enterprise is $20 per seat plus API usage. Copilot is $30 add-on plus $200 per 25k Studio credits. Gemini Enterprise lists $30–$50 (stale). ChatGPT Enterprise, Glean, Writer and all regional players are "Not published". Ralysa can price on platform plus department packs and pass model cost through. → **MA-107**
- **The biggest threat is a partnership of a hyperscaler suite and a sovereign operator,** such as Inception42 (G42) with Microsoft 365 Copilot interop (July 2026), Humain One on AWS Marketplace (May 2026) and Core42's Sovereign Public Cloud on Azure. → **MA-108, MA-109**

## Vendor profiles

### Microsoft 365 Copilot + Copilot Studio
- **Strengths:** It already sits on most Gulf enterprise desktops through M365 and Entra. UAE in-country Copilot processing is due in 2026. MCP is GA in Studio. Purview gives DLP, labels and audit out of the box. Studio lets you choose between OpenAI and Anthropic models. Arabic is a supported language. Microsoft 365 Copilot has been renamed "Microsoft Copilot" ([MS Learn, 2026-09-24](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/connect-to-ai-subprocessor)).
- **Gaps vs Ralysa:** No Copilot in the disconnected or on-prem sovereign stack. **Qatar and KSA are not on the in-country list.** Anthropic models fall outside in-country processing commitments, which undercuts BYO-model plus residency for T2/T3 data. Copilot Studio is low-code and M365-centred, not a Claude-Code-grade workspace with plan/act, files and code. It has no telecom packs (NOC, RA, Fraud). Credit-metered agents make costs hard to predict.

### ChatGPT Enterprise (+ Codex, Frontier)
- **Strengths:** Strong brand pull. UAE data residency plus **UAE inference residency (Aug 2026)**, which puts it ahead of Anthropic and Google in the UAE. Custom MCP apps with RBAC. Codex for developers. Frontier for enterprise agent management with named Fortune-500 launch customers ([TechCrunch, 2026-02-05](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/05/openai-launches-a-way-for-enterprises-to-build-and-manage-ai-agents/)).
- **Gaps vs Ralysa:** OpenAI models only. No on-prem or air-gapped option. No Qatar or KSA residency. Connectors may fall outside residency. Pricing is quote-only with high seat minimums (secondary). No department packs or telecom edition.

### Claude for Enterprise / Claude Code
- **Strengths:** The reference product Ralysa's positioning borrows from ("Claude Code for the whole enterprise"). Native MCP, skills, managed settings, Compliance API, CMEK. Low $20 seat plus usage. Claude Code can route to Bedrock, Vertex, Foundry or a self-hosted gateway.
- **Gaps vs Ralysa:** First-party data residency is **US or global only**, and Bedrock in the Middle East uses global cross-region inference. So Gulf T3 data has no in-country Claude path today (**Inference:** only a self-hosted model or a future in-region deployment closes this). No Arabic RTL UI is documented. No department packs.
- **Relationship note:** Ralysa plans to build on the Claude Agent SDK (spec §14). Anthropic is a **supplier and a competitor at once**. See MA-109.

### Google Gemini Enterprise (ex-Agentspace)
- **Strengths:** Broad connectors, no-code agent builder, governed MCP through Agent Gateway. Google Distributed Cloud air-gapped with Gemini is the only hyperscaler **air-gapped frontier model** we found. Doha and Dammam GCP regions exist, and the PIF AI hub partnership includes Arabic work on Gemini.
- **Gaps vs Ralysa:** Gemini Enterprise residency has **no Middle East location**. The air-gapped path is infrastructure plus models, not the Gemini Enterprise workspace (**Inference** from docs, to verify with Google). Gemini-first model strategy.

### Glean
- **Strengths:** Best-in-class permission-aware enterprise search. BYO keys through Model Hub. MCP both ways. Customer-hosted single-tenant in the customer's own cloud.
- **Gaps vs Ralysa:** Customer-hosted only on GCP or AWS, with no on-prem or air-gapped option. Customers can't patch or modify the service. **Arabic is Early access.** Search-first rather than action/agent-first for regulated back-office work. High price floor (secondary).

### Dust
- **Strengths:** Multi-model, MCP-native, cross-department agents. Transparent per-seat pricing. EU residency and single-tenant on Enterprise.
- **Gaps vs Ralysa:** US or EU residency only. No on-prem. Arabic not documented. Audit and SCIM only on Enterprise. Small vendor with no Gulf presence found.

### Writer
- **Strengths:** Owns its models (Palmyra X6, Aug 2026). MCP Gateway with private endpoints. Strong in marketing and revenue teams. Long-running agents. Mature compliance set.
- **Gaps vs Ralysa:** Palmyra-centric. Our self-hosting evidence is 3 years old (**stale**). Arabic not documented. No Gulf residency.

### G42 / Core42 / Inception42 (UAE)
- **Strengths:** Owns sovereign infrastructure in the UAE, including an Azure-based sovereign public cloud and in-country private cloud. Compass offers 50+ models including JAIS (Arabic). Catalyst (Inception42) is an agent OS with **two-way Microsoft 365 Copilot interoperability** (Jul 2026). Deep government relationships (vendor claim: 15+ government agencies).
- **Gaps vs Ralysa:** Platform and API first. No department packs or Claude-Code-style end-user workspace found. MCP not documented. UAE-centred.
- **Implication:** Core42 is more likely a **hosting and channel partner** than a head-on competitor.

### Humain (KSA)
- **Strengths:** PIF-backed. Humain One is the closest regional analogue to Ralysa: an Arabic-first agentic OS for HR, finance and procurement, sold through AWS Marketplace and with its own agent marketplace. It invested in MOZN (Aug 2026) to co-build financial-sector and public-sector agents.
- **Gaps vs Ralysa:** Governance, MCP, BYO-model and on-prem details are not documented. No telecom or network-operations depth found. Built around ALLaM and Humain's own systems of record.

### stc / SCCC (KSA) and Ooredoo / Syntys (Qatar)
- **Strengths:** In-country GPU clouds, and both are telcos, i.e. the same companies as Ralysa's beachhead buyers. stc offers Qwen/Llama/DeepSeek inference in the Kingdom. Ooredoo offers NIM-based MaaS and Rafay PaaS in Qatar.
- **Gaps vs Ralysa:** Infrastructure only. No governed department workspace found.
- **Implication:** Best treated as **hosting targets and possible resellers**, and as **customers**.

### e& + Open Innovation AI (UAE)
- **Strengths:** Fully on-prem and air-gapped stack (OI Chat, OI Agents, OI Code, Agent Fabric). Launched with the UAE Cyber Security Council as a national "Sovereign AI Platform" (May 2026). A security framework for validating agents is built in.
- **Gaps vs Ralysa:** Horizontal tooling. No department packs or SSO-group-driven access model documented. Arabic not documented.
- **Implication:** This is the **most direct sovereign threat in UAE government**.

### Arabic.AI (Tarjama)
- **Strengths:** Arabic-first model (Pronoia), dialect speech, OCR, 100+ assistants for legal, procurement and tenders. On-prem and air-gapped. Vendor claims 700+ clients.
- **Gaps vs Ralysa:** Assistant catalogue rather than an agent workspace with code, data and tools. MCP and BYO-model not documented.
- **Implication:** Possibly a **partner** for Arabic OCR, speech and translation skills.

## White space: where Ralysa can win

1. **An "Air-gapped Claude-Code-grade workspace for every department" in Qatar and KSA.** No global vendor offers an agent workspace that runs in-country in Qatar, or on-prem in KSA, with SSO-driven access today. Copilot can't run disconnected. OpenAI and Gemini Enterprise have no Qatar/KSA residency. Claude first-party is US or global. Glean and Dust are cloud-only. The regional players who can run on-prem (OI, Arabic.AI, Core42) lack Ralysa's depth in agent capabilities (skills, memory, hooks, subagents, code and data workspaces). (MA-101, MA-102, MA-104)
2. **Governance for T3 data that follows your identity provider.** The sensitivity tiers in spec §7.7 (T3 means isolated data, local models, no personal keys, full audit) are more specific than any competitor's published controls. The key message: **"the model allowed depends on your SSO group and the data tier."** Microsoft's own docs show that picking Anthropic in Copilot drops the in-country commitment, and Ralysa can make that trade-off explicit and enforceable. (MA-103, MA-109)
3. **Telecom Edition packs: NOC, Revenue Assurance, Fraud, TM Forum APIs.** No competitor publishes packaged agents for these functions (Assumption, based on reviewing the vendor material listed above). The Gulf telcos (stc, Ooredoo, e&) are both infrastructure competitors and the most natural first customers. (MA-105)
4. **Arabic document handling plus an RTL UI as a default, not an add-on,** compared with Glean (Early access), Dust and Writer (not documented). Pair it with an Arabic-specialist skill partner (e.g. Arabic.AI OCR and speech, or JAIS/ALLaM via BYO model). (MA-106)
5. **Neutral about models.** Ralysa can route T1 work to Claude or GPT, T2 work to in-region hyperscaler endpoints, and T3 work to local models (JAIS, ALLaM, Qwen, Llama) on stc, Ooredoo or Core42 GPUs. No single-model vendor can offer that routing. (MA-107)

## Threats

- **Hyperscaler catch-up.** Azure Saudi East opens in November 2026, and Microsoft could extend Copilot in-country processing to KSA and Qatar. OpenAI is expanding UAE compute residency. If Qatar and KSA residency arrives for Copilot or ChatGPT within 12 months, the "hosted where your data must stay" message weakens for T1/T2 data. (MA-101, MA-108)
- **Sovereign bundles.** Examples: Inception42 Catalyst with M365 Copilot interop, Humain One on AWS with a PIF-backed marketplace, and e&/OI with the UAE Cyber Security Council. National-champion bundles may be preferred or even mandated in government procurement. (MA-108)
- **Anthropic moving down-market into departments.** Cowork, connectors and a $20 seat could extend Claude Enterprise to business users. Ralysa also depends on the Claude Agent SDK and Anthropic's terms (spec §14). (MA-109)
- **MCP is commoditised.** Every major competitor supports MCP, so "MCP as universal connector" no longer differentiates on its own. (MA-103)
- **Price anchoring.** $20–$30 seat anchors (Claude, Copilot, Gemini) push buyers to expect low platform fees. (MA-107)

## Recommendations

- **Positioning:** "The only Claude-Code-grade agent workspace that runs in-country or air-gapped, and whose SSO groups decide the model, data and tools." Lead in **Qatar** (no global in-country option) and **KSA** telecom and banking. In the UAE, lead with on-prem, T3 and telecom packs rather than residency alone. (MA-101, MA-102, MA-109)
- **Pack priority:** Telecom Edition (Revenue Assurance, Fraud, NOC) plus Technology first. They are the least contested and closest to the telco beachhead. De-prioritise HR/Finance as lead packs in KSA, where Humain One is positioned. (MA-105, MA-110)
- **Pricing stance:** platform or deployment fee plus per-department-pack seat pricing, with model usage passed through at cost or covered by the customer's BYO key/endpoint. Publish a pilot price to stand out against the unpublished regional prices. (MA-107)
- **Roadmap changes:** (1) Make on-prem/air-gapped deployment and a local-model route for T3 Phase 1 must-haves. (2) Build *identity-scoped MCP governance* (allowlists per group, approvals, audit export) as the headline feature. (3) Ship an RTL UI and Arabic document handling in the first pilot. (4) Add Copilot/M365 interop (agent-to-agent or MCP) so Ralysa can sit next to Copilot. (MA-102, MA-103, MA-106, MA-108)
- **Partnerships:** approach one sovereign GPU/cloud partner per country (Ooredoo/Syntys, SCCC or Humain, Core42) as a hosting partner and possible reseller. Evaluate Arabic.AI or JAIS/ALLaM as Arabic-specialist models or skills available through BYO model. (MA-104, MA-108)
